# Wrenchly · Discovery

> Fictional example. Wrenchly, its documents and numbers, the product manager's answers and the score they overturned are invented, tagged `[PM, illustrative]`. Market facts cite the reports in this folder or a public source retrieved on 27 Oct 2026.

Is the product ready to go where the vision says? Built from `protection.md`, `strength.md`, `exposure.md`, `value.md`, `../vision/vision.md` and the product manager's answers on 27 Oct 2026.

## In short

Wrenchly is strong inside the fleet's own shop: deeply vertical, embedded in daily work orders, parts and DOT records, and loyal larger customers. It is exposed because the telematics vendors and Fleetio shipped maintenance AI this year, are winning smaller customers, and supply the data our own AI depends on. The move is **Move first**: protect the shop's work for fleets of 150 to 500 vehicles, and go fast on turning fault codes, invoices and notes into ready work, starting by learning exactly how managers triage fault codes today.

## Where to take the product

- **Protect:** the shop's own work for fleets of 150 to 500 vehicles with their own shops: multi-shop parts, DOT records and edge cases, where churn is 4% and we win half our deals (`protection.md`, [documents/churn-note-2026.md], [documents/lost-deals-h1-2026.md]). Give the 37 customers facing a bundled offer a business review before renewal.
- **Grow:**
  1. *From fault code to a ready work order.* Value comes from keeping customers the bundles target, then a per-vehicle shop agent (`../vision/opportunities.md` #1, `value.md`).
  2. *Outside invoices read and checked.* Value comes from the buyer's savings on outside repairs, which the buyer says "would pay for itself in a quarter" (`../vision/opportunities.md` #2).
  3. *Warranty money found and claimed.* Value comes from a share of money recovered, a new revenue line (`../vision/opportunities.md` #5).
- **Start with:** the problem that every morning, maintenance managers and lead technicians read hundreds of fault codes one by one to find the few trucks that need work, then type the work orders themselves [documents/interview-maintenance-manager-regional-carrier.md]. Before building anything, learn from the beta customers how many codes they see a day, how many become work, what they check before opening a work order, what the technician later found, and what they'd need to see to approve a draft without checking. `[PM, illustrative]`
- **Don't:**
  - Don't build benchmarks or cross-fleet predictions until legal confirms the contract rights and technicians' notes are good enough to learn from (`strength.md`).
  - Don't chase fleets under 75 vehicles on price: we win a third of those deals and lose most churn there (`../vision/market-moves.md`).
  - Don't rebuild the monolith first; build the new AI work as services next to it. `[PM, illustrative]`
- **How we'll know:**
  - The share of drafted work orders approved without edits, and how often the draft matched what the technician found.
  - Renewals among the 37 customers with a bundled offer.
  - How many beta customers say yes to paying for the shop agent, and at what price.

## Where we're strong

- Built only for fleet maintenance, and the system of record for the DOT inspection and repair records fleets must keep (`strength.md`, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.3).
- Larger customers stay: churn is 4% above 150 vehicles and 3% where parts run in two or more shops [documents/churn-note-2026.md].
- Fifteen years of edge cases across carriers, distributors, waste haulers, utilities and last-mile fleets (`protection.md`).
- About 2 million work orders a year across 800 fleets, a latent asset buyers already ask for `[PM, illustrative]` [documents/lost-deals-h1-2026.md].
- A healthy market with stable rules: fleet management is forecast to grow about 17% a year to 2035 (https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/fleet-management-market).

## Where we're exposed

- **The protection eroding fastest: switching costs.** Contracts are annual, rivals onboard fast, and AI makes moving records and retraining cheaper (`protection.md`).
- **The highest risk: AI disruption.** Motive Maintenance turns defects and fault codes into work orders automatically (https://gomotive.com/motive-launches-ai-powered-maintenance-to-help-operations-teams-prevent-breakdowns-increase-uptime-and-lower-repair-costs/), and 31 customers left for telematics bundles in a year [documents/churn-note-2026.md].
- **Dependence on rivals' data.** The fault codes and meter readings our AI needs come from Samsara, Motive and Geotab, who also sell maintenance (`exposure.md`).
- **Feature parity.** Motive matched most of our feature set in one release (`protection.md`).

## The AI opportunity

- **AI in the product today:** level 1, System of Record, with a level 2 beta (`../vision/ai-levels.md`).
- **Where AI could earn money:**
  - A per-vehicle shop agent that drafts work orders from codes and defects (`../vision/opportunities.md` #1).
  - A share of warranty money recovered (`../vision/opportunities.md` #5).
  - Later, benchmarks for "fleets like us" (`../vision/opportunities.md` #6).
- **Where AI could take work off customers:**
  - Fault-code triage, an hour a morning for one lead tech.
  - Technician notes, an estimated 20–30 minutes per job.
  - Outside invoices, typed line by line.
  - Parts ordering.
  - The cost-per-mile report, two days a month.
  - Sources: [documents/interview-maintenance-manager-regional-carrier.md] [documents/interview-fleet-ops-director-distribution.md]

## Our move

| | Lower risk | Higher risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Higher AI opportunity** | Build on it | **Move first** |
| **Lower AI opportunity** | Optimise | Defend |

We are in **Move first**: AI opportunity 3.67, risk 3.0. The risk score is borderline: it sits on the 3.0 line, and one point on any single risk would move it across.

The opportunity is clearly above the line. The work AI could take off customers is large, and it's the shop's own work, where Wrenchly is deepest. The risk is on the line because the market and rules are safe, but rivals are shipping AI in the same workflow now. For us, Move first means doing the shop's paperwork with AI before the bundles reach the fleets of 150 to 500 vehicles.

## Ready for the vision?

The vision: level 4 by the end of 2028, starting with "from fault code to a ready work order" (`../vision/vision.md`, `../vision/opportunities.md` #1).

| | What we have | The gap | Must be true | How we'll check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Data** | 15 years of work orders and parts; fault codes from about 310 customers [documents/fault-code-assist-beta-feedback.md] | Technicians' notes rarely say what they found, so we can't check accuracy; 30% of customers are on older contracts; the telematics feeds belong to rivals | We capture what each code actually led to, and our agreements with telematics vendors allow AI use of their feeds | Sample closed work orders at beta customers for cause and correction; legal reviews the 2021 contract and the telematics data terms `[PM, illustrative]` |
| **Tech** | Newer .NET 8 services, a public API, the Fault Code Assist beta on a general model `[PM, illustrative]` | The monolith and meter sync, the top support issue at 18% [documents/support-tickets-q3-2026.csv] | Drafting work orders can run as a service beside the monolith, with reliable meter data | Engineering lead checks the work-order and parts interfaces the agent needs `[PM, illustrative]` |
| **Team** | 34 people in product and engineering; implementation specialists who ran fleet shops `[PM, illustrative]` | No one with experience shipping AI agents or measuring their accuracy `[PM, illustrative]` | One person owns AI quality and evaluation | Agree the role with the general manager and the group's AI guild `[PM, illustrative]` |
| **Customers** | 46 beta customers, 31 active, 61% thumbs-up; customers asking for more [documents/fault-code-assist-beta-feedback.md] | We don't know how often managers would approve a draft unchanged | Managers approve drafts without rework most of the time | The problem interviews in "Start with", then a pilot with beta customers |
| **Pricing** | Per-vehicle pricing customers understand [documents/product-one-pager.md] | No AI price tested; Fleetio sells at $4–$10 per vehicle (https://www.fleetio.com/pricing) | Buyers pay for the shop agent, or renew because of it | Test included-in-Pro against an add-on with beta customers |

**Builds on:** vertical depth and embeddedness in the shop, 15 years of edge cases and repair history, and loyal larger customers (`strength.md`, `protection.md`).

**Could stop it:** a telematics vendor limiting its data feed; bundles reaching larger fleets before we ship; drafts too inaccurate for managers to trust (`exposure.md`).

## Where value flows

In short, from `value.md`:

| | Today | At the vision's target level |
|---|---|---|
| **Who gets value** | The fleet gets a well-kept record; managers, technicians and office staff still do the work around it | Maintenance managers get ready work orders; buyers get overcharges caught and warranty money back |
| **Who pays, and for what** | The buyer, per vehicle per month: $8–$13, trailers $3 [documents/product-one-pager.md] | The buyer, per vehicle for the record and the shop agent, plus a share of warranty recovered `[PM, illustrative]` |
| **Where value leaks** | Manual fault-code triage, notes, invoices, missed warranty, Excel reports; telematics bundles | Telematics vendors and general assistants, if they do the work on our records |
| **Who else could capture it** | Motive, Samsara, Fleetio | Motive and Samsara agents; Fleetio; ChatGPT, Claude and Copilot through connectors |
| **What keeps it with us** | Parts depth, DOT records, people who know shops | The shop's workflow, data from all telematics providers, trust in the DOT record |

**The shift:** paying per vehicle for a record people type into → paying for trucks back on the road sooner and money recovered.
