# Customer interview: maintenance manager, regional LTL carrier

*Fictional. Notes from a customer call by the Wrenchly product team, 9 Sep 2026. Names removed.*

**Who:** maintenance manager at a regional less-than-truckload carrier in the Midwest. 240 tractors, 610 trailers, two shops, 14 technicians, one parts manager. Wrenchly customer since 2017, on the Pro plan. Trucks run Samsara telematics.

**Interviewers:** product manager, designer.

## Their week

- Starts every morning with the overnight DVIRs and the fault-code list. "I've got 40 driver write-ups and a few hundred fault codes waiting before I've had coffee. Most of the codes are noise. Two of them will be the truck that breaks down on I-70 tonight, and I can't tell which two."
- Builds the day's shop schedule on a whiteboard, then types it into Wrenchly. Dispatch changes it twice before noon. "The PM schedule is right in Wrenchly. The trucks just aren't where Wrenchly thinks they are."
- Measured on: road calls (breakdowns on the road) per million miles, PM compliance, out-of-service rate at roadside inspections, cost per mile.

## What works

- PM schedules by mileage and engine hours, synced from Samsara. "Before Wrenchly we did PMs off a wall calendar and missed a third of them."
- The DOT annual inspection records and the audit report. "When the auditor came in 2024 I printed the file in ten minutes. The year before Wrenchly that took a week."
- Parts inventory across two shops. Better than what they saw in demos of simpler tools.

## What hurts

- **Fault codes.** Samsara sends codes into Wrenchly, but someone has to read each one, look it up, decide if it matters and open a work order. "My lead tech spends the first hour of his day as a fault-code clerk."
- **Technician notes.** Techs type "replaced" or "fixed" and close the work order. Cause and correction are missing, so warranty claims get rejected and repeat repairs are hard to spot. "Half my techs would rather turn a wrench for two hours than type for ten minutes." They estimate techs spend 20 to 30 minutes per job on notes, time entry and finding the right part number.
- **Warranty.** No one has time to check whether a failed part is still under warranty. The maintenance manager thinks they recover "maybe a third" of what they could.
- **Rescheduling.** When a truck misses its PM slot because dispatch kept it on a route, nothing reschedules it. The planner finds out when the truck is overdue.
- **Outside repairs.** Roadside repairs and dealer invoices arrive as PDFs by email. The office manager types them in, line by line.

## Quotes

- "Don't give me another dashboard. Give me the three trucks I need to pull in today, and why."
- "If it wrote the work order from the fault code and the tech just checked it, I'd pay for that."
- "Samsara keeps telling me they do maintenance now. Their maintenance is a checklist. But my CFO sees one bill instead of two."

## Follow-ups

- Share the Fault Code Assist beta with them.
- Ask if they would share de-identified repair history for benchmarks (they asked how their cost per mile compares to "fleets like us").
