## What it does

Sales Cloud keeps a company's accounts, contacts, leads and deals in one place. Sellers track their pipeline, managers forecast revenue, and leaders manage quotas and commissions. It captures calls, emails and meetings, and connects to quoting, contracts and billing. [https://www.salesforce.com/sales/]

Service Cloud runs customer service. It manages cases, knowledge articles and assets, connects voice and digital channels in a contact center, and covers field service. [https://www.salesforce.com/service/]

Both now ship with AI agents under the Agentforce name. They qualify leads, answer customers and resolve cases. Since September 2026 the range also includes Fin (formerly Intercom), a customer service agent Salesforce acquired. [https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/09/10/salesforce-completes-acquisition-of-fin/]

### Account executive at a mid-market B2B company

- who they are: a quota-carrying seller at a software or services company with a few hundred employees. A user, not the buyer. `[PM, illustrative]`
- what they're trying to get done: work the right deals, prepare for meetings, move opportunities forward and hit quota.
- the problems this product solves for them: one view of each account and its history; deal tracking that flags risk before a deal stalls. [https://www.salesforce.com/sales/]
- how they handled it before, or would without the product: spreadsheets, inbox folders and memory, or a lighter CRM. `[PM, illustrative]`
- what still frustrates them: reviewers report a steep learning curve and say some setups still need admin help. [https://g2.com/products/salesforce-crm/reviews] Research before calls takes time: Carnegie Learning reported rep research falling from up to an hour to 5–10 minutes once an agent did it, which shows how long it took before. [https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/agentforce-customer-success-stories/]

### Sales manager or VP of sales

- who they are: leads a team of 8–40 sellers. Often the buyer, together with revenue operations. `[PM, illustrative]`
- what they're trying to get done: a forecast leadership can trust, healthy pipeline coverage, and coaching that improves win rates.
- the problems this product solves for them: forecasting from live deal data, quota and commission tracking, performance data for coaching. [https://www.salesforce.com/sales/]
- how they handled it before: weekly pipeline calls and forecast roll-ups in spreadsheets. `[PM, illustrative]`
- what still frustrates them: a forecast is only as good as what reps type in. `[PM, illustrative]` Licence and add-on costs rise as needs grow. [https://g2.com/products/salesforce-crm/reviews]

### Salesforce admin or revenue operations lead

- who they are: configures Salesforce, builds reports and flows, manages permissions and data quality. Influences the purchase; often owns the renewal. `[PM, illustrative]`
- what they're trying to get done: keep the system matching how the business sells and serves, with clean data and working reports.
- the problems this product solves for them: one configurable platform for sales and service data, workflows and reporting. [https://www.salesforce.com/sales/]
- how they handled it before: separate tools stitched together, or custom code. `[PM, illustrative]`
- what still frustrates them: reviewers single out permission setup and advanced automation and reporting as hard. [https://g2.com/products/salesforce-crm/reviews] Capterra reviewers are mostly negative on value for money and point to extra costs for features, support and storage. [https://capterra.com/p/61368/Salesforce/reviews/]

### Customer service rep and service manager

- who they are: reps answering customers by chat, email and phone, and the manager who runs the team. The manager or head of service buys; reps use. `[PM, illustrative]`
- what they're trying to get done: resolve cases fast and well, keep satisfaction up and cost per case down.
- the problems this product solves for them: cases, knowledge and channels in one console; AI agents that take routine questions. Engine's agent resolves half of incoming chat cases and hands the rest to a rep with the history attached. [https://www.salesforce.com/customer-stories/engine/agentic-service/]
- how they handled it before: shared inboxes, phone queues and a helpdesk tool. `[PM, illustrative]`
- what still frustrates them: getting set up depends on consultants, according to reviewers. [https://capterra.com/p/136189/Salesforce/reviews/] Complex cases still need people: Engine's agent hands off large group rebookings to a rep. [https://www.salesforce.com/customer-stories/engine/agentic-service/]

## Main workflows

- Lead to qualified opportunity: capture, score, contact, qualify, book a meeting. Agentforce Engagement (formerly the SDR agent) now does much of this on its own. [https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=sales.sales_agent_sdr_parent.htm&language=en_US&type=5]
- Opportunity to close: account research, meetings, activity logging, deal updates, forecast. [https://www.salesforce.com/sales/]
- Case intake to resolution: a customer asks on any channel; an agent or rep answers, acts and closes the case. [https://www.salesforce.com/service/]
- Admin and reporting: configuration, permissions, reports and dashboards. [https://g2.com/products/salesforce-crm/reviews]

## What customers love / complain about

- love: centralised data, automation and flexibility to customise (G2's summary of 25,000+ reviews, rated 4.4). [https://g2.com/products/salesforce-crm/reviews] Sales and activity tracking is the most praised feature on Capterra. [https://capterra.com/p/61368/Salesforce/reviews/]
- complain: complexity and a steep learning curve; cost and add-ons; storage pricing; some integration gaps that cause double entry. [https://g2.com/products/salesforce-crm/reviews] [https://capterra.com/p/61368/Salesforce/reviews/]

## Pricing and market

- Sales Cloud and Service Cloud list prices, per user per month, billed annually: Starter Suite $25, Pro Suite $100, Core $195, Advanced $395, Max $550 (Max includes the full Agentforce suite and 2.75M Flex Credits per org per year). [https://www.salesforce.com/sales/pricing/] [https://www.salesforce.com/service/pricing/]
- Agentforce: add-ons for Sales or Service at $125 per user per month; Flex Credits at $500 per 100,000 credits (an action costs 20 credits, about $0.10); or $2 per conversation for customer-facing agents. [https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/pricing/]
- Agentforce Help Agent, launched June 2026, charges only when the agent resolves an issue on its own; trade press reports $2 per resolution. [https://www.cxnetwork.com/cx-experience/news/salesforce-launches-pay-per-resolution-agent/amp] [https://communicateonline.me/news/salesforce-launches-agentforce-help-agent-with-pay-per-resolution-pricing/]
- Company revenue was $41.5B in fiscal 2026 (to Jan 2026). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salesforce] Q2 fiscal 2027 revenue was $11.3B, up 11%. Agentforce annual recurring revenue passed $1.5B, now counting Slackbot and Headless 360. Sales and Service are not reported separately. [https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Delivers-Record-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027-Results/default.aspx]
- Salesforce says it has led Gartner's Magic Quadrant for CRM sales platforms for 20 years in a row. [https://www.salesforce.com/sales/]

## Data and tech

- Holds accounts, contacts, leads, opportunities, activities, cases and knowledge for each customer company, often going back many years. [https://www.salesforce.com/sales/]
- Data 360 (formerly Data Cloud) unifies customer data from Salesforce and other sources, such as Snowflake and Amazon S3 in Engine's case. [https://www.salesforce.com/customer-stories/engine/agentic-service/]
- Headless 360 opens the platform to outside agents through APIs, MCP servers and a command line. Agents in Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor can call Salesforce actions (expanded August 2026). [https://www.destinationcrm.com/Articles/CRM-News/CRM-Across-the-Wire/Salesforce-Expands-Headless-360-Across-Its-Portfolio-176206.aspx]

## AI today

- Einstein features suggest and score: lead and opportunity scoring, forecasting, call summaries, record summaries. [https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=sales.sales_core_artificial_intelligence.htm&language=en_US&type=5]
- Agentforce agents act on their own: they nurture and qualify leads and resolve service conversations. On Salesforce's own help site they have handled more than five million conversations and resolved over 63% of them instantly. [https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/case-study/customer-zero]
- 7.0 billion "agentic work units" (discrete tasks agents completed) delivered to date across Agentforce and Slack, 3.2 billion of them in Q2 fiscal 2027. [https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Delivers-Record-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027-Results/default.aspx]

## The idea we have in mind

Sellers still spend time keeping the CRM up to date instead of selling. We want Sales Cloud to keep its own records, and to tell each team what is working in deals like theirs. `[PM, illustrative]`

- The right problem: sales managers can't see where every deal really stands without chasing reps. BHAG: by 2031, sellers spend 70% of their week selling, up from 40%, and no manager has to ask where a deal stands. See `problem/problem.md`. `[PM, illustrative]`
- This product update: the rep confirms each deal change the agent proposes, and the manager's forecast brief is built only from what reps confirmed (flows 1 and 2 in `flows/flows.md`); $45 a user a month, a fixed line at renewal; build-day slice: one team's sample data, from a call to a proposed change, the rep's confirmation, the brief and an override with a reason. See `roadmap/roadmap.md`. `[PM, illustrative]`
