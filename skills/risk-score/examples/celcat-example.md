# Celcat — Risk Score Report

## 1. Risk Summary

Company:         Celcat (celcat.com)

Not borderline — 2.73 sits comfortably below the 3.0 threshold. The composite reads as moderately protected overall, but that headline masks a real split: Celcat's product-defensibility and business-health dimensions score consistently low-risk (2s), while both end-market dimensions tied to the health of its core buyer base — university budgets and the international-student-visa regime that funds them — score 4. This is a company with strong structural moats sitting inside a genuinely stressed market.

## 2. Top Risks

**1. Market Growth — Score 4 (Structural) — "The buyer base is in a funding crisis, not just the market"**
- An Office for Students update projects 45% of English higher-education providers ending 2025-26 in deficit; over 12,000 UK higher-ed jobs were cut in 2025, with 105 institutions restructuring.
- International student visa grants — the main revenue engine for many of Celcat's institutional customers — are down sharply across its core geographies: UK applications down roughly 33% in early 2026, Canada down 65% year-over-year, Australia down 12%.
- Category-level analyst forecasts for academic scheduling software still show healthy growth (11–14% CAGR), but that TAM-level optimism doesn't reflect the budget reality of the institutions actually signing Celcat's contracts.

**2. Regulatory & Market Shift Risk — Score 4 (Near-term) — "Simultaneous visa-policy tightening across every core market"**
- UKVI is actively tightening international-student attendance-compliance rules (from April 2026, a class register alone no longer satisfies sponsor duties), while a new international-tuition levy takes effect from January 2026.
- Equivalent restrictive shifts are happening concurrently in Australia and Canada — the three markets where Celcat and its regional partner CDS (Australasia) operate.
- This cuts two ways: tighter compliance rules increase near-term demand for Celcat's attendance-monitoring tooling, but the same policy wave is shrinking the international-student population that tooling exists to track — a real, unresolved tension rather than a one-directional tailwind.

**3. AI Disruption — Score 3 (Medium-term) — "Well-funded AI-native competitors are already live, just not yet in Celcat's backyard"**
- Coursedog has raised $113M+ (including a $90M JMI Equity round) and serves 170+ institutions with an AI-powered academic operations platform; Ad Astra brands itself the "only Smart Scheduling software for Higher Ed" and cites $720M in cumulative institutional financial impact through 2024.
- Celcat's own installed-base depth (200+ institutions, 30+ countries, 45+ years), deep SIS/HR/payroll integrations, and UK-specific UKVI-compliance tooling are genuine complementary assets that neither competitor currently replicates — both are more US-centric — which caps this score rather than pushing it higher on category hype alone.
- Celcat shipped its own answer (Celcat AI, an agentic "cohort of timetabling agents") in November 2025, indicating the competitive pressure was real enough to force a ground-up product response.

## 3. Risk Landscape

**End Market Environment.** This section carries the report's real weight. Market growth and regulatory/market-shift risk both land at 4, driven by the same underlying fact: Celcat's core customer base (UK, Australian, and Canadian higher-education institutions) is under simultaneous financial and immigration-policy stress, even while the abstract global market for academic scheduling software keeps growing on paper. AI-disruption risk (3) is real but not yet displacing Celcat specifically — the competitive threat is more advanced in the US market than in Celcat's home territories. Partner/ecosystem dependency (2) is comparatively low: Celcat's integration footprint spans many SIS/HR vendors (Tribal SITS, Ellucian Banner, Unit4, StarRez, iTrent) rather than concentrating on one platform. This is the section to watch for acceleration — the visa and funding pressures are recent and still unfolding through 2026, not settled history.

**Product Defensibility.** Consistently low-risk. Deep, ERP-style integrations into SIS, HR, and payroll systems (scored 2 on integration/lock-in depth) and a compliance-driven substitution barrier tied to UKVI visa-sponsor attendance monitoring (also 2) both point toward hard-to-leave positioning, even without visibility into actual contract terms. Timing of disruption (3) is the one dimension in this section trending toward exposure — AI-native competitors are shipping and generating measurable outcomes now, not on some distant horizon, even if actual customer displacement in Celcat's territory hasn't been observed yet.

**Business Health.** The most inference-heavy section, and appropriately cautious as a result. Price-value pressure (3) reflects a real conflict: Celcat's opaque, quote-based pricing looks like normal enterprise-sales behavior for a 45-year-old product, not weakness, but the sector-wide budget crisis raises the odds of renewal friction regardless. Audit/approvals embeddedness (2) is stronger than it might first appear — the Staff Pay Claim module runs configurable multi-stage approval workflows tied to payroll disbursement, and attendance data feeds UKVI compliance audits directly. Brand and trust (2) rests on structural signals (installed base, named case studies, Volaris/Constellation backing) rather than review-site behavioral data, because none of substance exists. Tech-stack obsolescence (3) reflects a genuinely mixed estate: a legacy Windows desktop client still shipping alongside a new cloud-native AI product line.

## 4. Watch-outs

- **Strong installed base, weakening buyer solvency.** Celcat's 45-year track record and deep integrations suggest a "safe" customer relationship, but if a meaningful share of that installed base is itself financially distressed (45% of English providers projected into deficit), retention metrics could look healthy right up until a wave of non-renewals or downgrades hits — the classic lagging-indicator trap.
- **Compliance tooling as both shield and exposure.** The UKVI attendance-compliance tie-in is currently a demand driver, but if international student numbers keep falling at the pace seen in early 2026, the addressable market for that specific feature could shrink materially even as the compliance requirement itself gets stricter.
- **Near-zero independent review signal on a 200+ institution, 45-year-old product.** That combination is unusual — either satisfied customers simply don't post reviews for enterprise-procured back-office software, or there's a genuine gap in structured customer-voice data that diligence should close directly with references.
- **AI-native competitors are ahead on paper but not yet in Celcat's territory.** Coursedog and Ad Astra have real funding and real customer outcomes, but both skew US-centric — worth confirming directly whether either has begun actively selling into UK/European/Australian higher ed, where Celcat's installed base sits.

## 5. Open Questions for Interview

1. **Price-value pressure (R9):** "Have any customers requested pricing renegotiation, contract downsizing, or delayed renewal in the last 12 months, tied explicitly to institutional budget pressure?" — an affirmative answer would move this from 3 toward 4.
2. **Substitution & data portability (R6):** "What do actual customer contracts specify for term length, exit notice, and data-export obligations on termination?" — direct contract terms (currently unavailable publicly) could move this score in either direction and would sharply increase confidence, currently Low/inference-heavy.
3. **Tech stack obsolescence (R12):** "What percentage of the current 200+ institution base is still on the legacy Windows desktop client versus migrated to Celcat AI / Timetabler Connect, and what is the migration timeline for the remainder?" — a high legacy-client share would push this toward 4; a near-complete migration would pull it toward 2.
4. **Market growth (R2):** "Has renewal or expansion revenue from UK/Australian/Canadian institutions specifically shown any measurable slowdown in the last two renewal cycles?" — would validate or moderate the structural read currently inferred from sector-wide OfS/ICEF data rather than Celcat-specific figures.
5. **AI disruption (R1):** "Has Celcat lost a competitive evaluation to Coursedog, Ad Astra, or another AI-native scheduling vendor in the UK, Europe, or Australia in the past 18 months?" — a confirmed loss would be the strongest available signal to move this above the current complementary-asset-capped 3.

## 6. Methodology Note

Research covered roughly 20 WebSearch/WebFetch queries across all three sections: End Market Environment (~7 searches covering AI-native competitors, market-size forecasts, UK/Australia/Canada visa and higher-ed funding data, and UKVI regulatory detail), Product Defensibility (~5 searches on integrations, SIS/HR partner ecosystems, and migration/case-study evidence), and Business Health (~8 searches/fetches on pricing, review platforms — Capterra, SoftwareAdvice, G2, TrustRadius — careers/tech-stack signals, and ownership history). No usable review volume was found on G2, Capterra, or TrustRadius (one review apiece on Capterra/SoftwareAdvice, G2 page inaccessible) — brand/trust and price-value scores are anchored to structural indicators (installed base, named case studies, quote-based enterprise pricing norms) rather than demonstrated behavioral review outcomes, per the zero-review-platform convention. R7 (AI Labor Displacement Risk) is retired under framework v2.0 and was not scored.
