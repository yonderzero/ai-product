## 2. Verdict

Celcat is a structurally strong, deeply vertical FE/HE scheduling vendor that behaves like a well-embedded incumbent in its real market niche rather than the "world leader" its own marketing claims — a defined sub-segment (further education plus smaller, non-centralised higher-education institutions) it does not dominate against the UK HE duopoly of Scientia/TechnologyOne and CMIS/OneAdvanced. Its single strongest asset is customer embeddedness: long-tenured, multi-system-integrated relationships (University of East London since 2010) where the product is wired into attendance, HR, and space-management data that institutions would find genuinely disruptive to unwind. Its biggest maturity gap is commercial: leadership has funded and shipped a genuinely modern, agentic AI-native platform (Celcat AI, launched November 2025) well ahead of its direct category rivals, but there is no visible pricing model or monetization strategy to capture that investment as new revenue yet.

## 4. Section Analysis

### Section A (Business Quality) — 3.83/5.0

In practice, Celcat operates as the authoritative record of a specific but consequential slice of institutional operations — the master academic timetable — feeding data outward into student records, HR, and space-management systems rather than merely consuming it, and the product is built entirely around FE/HE-specific concepts (modules, cohorts, exam boards, teaching weeks) rather than adapted from a horizontal base. The strongest dimension is vertical specificity: there is no evidence of horizontal reuse, and case studies show real workflow depth (attendance data tied directly to a documented dropout-rate improvement at University of East London). The weakest dimension is technology modernity: the still-dominant installed base runs on an older Windows client/SQL Server architecture, and while the new Celcat AI platform is genuinely modern (cloud, graph database, agentic), it launched only in November 2025 and has not yet displaced the legacy footprint. Market position is a real but contained asset — an independent HE-sector consultancy source places Celcat as the go-to vendor for further education and smaller, non-centralised institutions, not as a challenger to the Scientia/CMIS duopoly that holds roughly three-quarters of the UK HE market.

### Section B (Customer Strength) — 4.00/5.0

Customer strength looks like a genuinely sticky, long-tenure incumbent relationship rather than a thin subscription: the University of East London has been a Celcat customer since 2010, on a site-wide licence with deep bidirectional wiring into HR, MIS, digital signage, and RFID attendance systems. Switching propensity and workflow embeddedness score identically and for largely the same reason — long tenure plus multi-system integration are structural signals, not just customer sentiment, and per the framework's evidence hierarchy that is what justifies scoring above the behavioral midpoint. Neither dimension reaches the top anchor because no churn, win/loss, or renewal-rate data was found to confirm the behavior at scale beyond the case studies surfaced. The evidence anchoring this section is thin in variety — it rests almost entirely on Celcat's own published case studies (University of East London, Keele, Walsall College) rather than independent reviews describing switching difficulty directly.

### Section C (AI Opportunity) — 3.75/5.0

The AI picture is a company that has clearly moved — Celcat AI is a real, shipped, multi-agent, policy-driven scheduling platform, not a slideware roadmap — but hasn't yet built a commercial engine around what it built. Current AI features is the strongest dimension of the three that aren't upside-only: the November 2025 launch of a genuinely agentic architecture (graph database, cohort of specialised agents, scenario modelling) is real, customer-facing capability, tempered only by its youth (roughly eight months old at scoring time) and the absence of independent confirmation of live usage outcomes. AI revenue opportunity is the clear weak point: no AI-specific pricing tier, usage-based pricing, or disclosed AI revenue was found anywhere, meaning the commercial model has not caught up to the product. Labor automation upside is the standout at the top anchor, because timetabling itself is one of the most iterative, multi-constraint, and currently manual workflows in FE/HE administration, and Celcat's own agentic architecture is purpose-built to absorb exactly that labor.

## 5. AI Picture

Read together, the four AI dimensions describe a company that is ahead of the market calibration anchors on shipping but behind on monetization. Against the Houlihan Lokey stage anchors, a shipped, agentic, multi-agent architecture launched company-wide at a dedicated conference pushes current AI features toward the "Scaling" band rather than "Emerging" — genuinely ahead of where most of the market (46% still Emerging) sits, and notably ahead of Celcat's own direct category rivals (Scientia/TechnologyOne, CMIS/OneAdvanced), for whom no evidence of comparable shipped AI scheduling capability was found in the last 12 months. That relative lead is also what keeps initiative urgency comfortable rather than pressing — Celcat isn't chasing a competitor's AI launch, it set the pace in its own segment. But AI revenue opportunity sits at Stage 1 of the monetization ladder (subscription/seat-based, no distinct AI line item), a real gap given the product investment already made. The labor-automation upside sits squarely in the agentic layer, not the ML/forecasting layer — autonomous, multi-step conflict resolution and closed-loop scenario modelling across rooms, staff and constraints — which the framework treats as a higher, harder-to-commoditise ceiling once genuinely embedded in customer workflows. No evidence surfaced of a competitor already shipping automation into that same labor-displacement surface ahead of Celcat; if anything, the opposite reading applies here.

## 6. Strategic Priorities

1. **Build a monetization model for Celcat AI before the shipping lead erodes.** Why now: the product investment (a full agentic rebuild) is already made and ahead of direct rivals, but there is no visible pricing tier or usage-based model to capture it — that gap is Celcat's most correctable weakness and the one most exposed to a fast-following competitor closing the shipping gap while Celcat is still figuring out how to charge for what it built. Good outcome in 12 months: a defined premium tier or add-on pricing structure for Celcat AI capabilities, distinct from the base Timetabler subscription, with early paying-customer proof points. Owner: commercial/product leadership (the Product Manager function, in coordination with the CEO given the scale of the platform decision).
2. **Convert the Celcat AI case-study gap into real evidence.** Why now: every quantified customer outcome found (UEL's dropout-rate improvement, Walsall's payroll transformation) predates the AI-native platform and describes the legacy product — the new platform's own claims currently rest on company material alone. Good outcome in 12 months: at least two to three published, named customer case studies specifically documenting Celcat AI (not legacy Timetabler) outcomes, with quantified results, to convert marketing claims into independently checkable evidence. Owner: marketing/customer success, sourcing from early Celcat AI adopter institutions.
3. **Decide deliberately whether to compete for the centralised-HE segment or defend the FE/smaller-HE niche.** Why now: the market position read shows Celcat is not a challenger to the Scientia/CMIS duopoly in large centralised universities, and the AI-native rebuild is a natural moment to either double down on defensible niche leadership or make a real push upmarket — drifting without a decision risks spending R&D on a platform aimed at a market segment sales isn't positioned to win. Good outcome in 12 months: a stated, resourced segment strategy (documented target sub-segment, competitive positioning against named rivals) rather than continued "world leader" messaging that independent sources don't support. Owner: CEO/executive leadership, given it is a market-strategy decision, not a product one.

## 7. Watch-outs

1. Is the legacy Windows-client/SQL Server architecture still running the bulk of the 200+-institution installed base, and if so, what is the actual migration timeline to Celcat AI — the technology modernity score rests on inference from dated technical documentation, not a confirmed current-state audit.
2. How much real customer usage does Celcat AI have eight months after launch — is it in production at a meaningful number of institutions, or still substantially in early-adopter pilot, given no independent confirmation of live deployment was found?
3. Does Celcat actually hold a defensible position within the further-education/smaller-HE niche specifically, or is even that narrower claim resting mostly on Celcat's own "market leader" language rather than independent confirmation — the market position score assumed the niche framing was accurate without independent verification of within-niche standing?
4. Given the AI revenue opportunity and AI labor-automation-upside scores diverge sharply (a 2 next to a 5), is there an internal commercial plan already in motion that wasn't visible in public sources, or is this genuinely an open gap between what's been built and what's been priced?
