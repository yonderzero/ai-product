# The six levels: from system of record to system of understanding

Each level describes **one product capability**, not a whole company. A company usually has features at several levels; place each feature, then place the company at the **highest level it has shipped and customers use**.

| # | Level | Who does the work | What it looks like | Evidence that it's real |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **System of Record** | People do the work; software stores it | Records, workflows, forms, reports. No AI, or AI only in marketing copy | Product pages and docs describe storage, workflow and reporting; no AI features in docs or release notes |
| 2 | **AI Assisted** | People do the work; AI suggests | Autocomplete, summaries, smart defaults, "suggested next step", search that understands questions | Release notes or docs for features that suggest, summarise or classify inside the existing screens; a person still clicks every step |
| 3 | **AI Directed** | A person asks; AI does one task; the person checks each output | "Draft this", "build me a report", "fill this form from that document", a copilot that acts on instruction | A copilot or assistant that takes instructions and produces work (documents, records, configurations), with the user reviewing each result |
| 4 | **AI Delegated** | AI does the job end to end; a person handles exceptions and approves | Agents that resolve tickets, process documents, schedule, reconcile, write the note; humans review exceptions | Metrics like "resolves X% of tickets", "processes invoices without touch", outcome- or usage-based pricing per resolution, approval queues in the docs |
| 5 | **AI Domain Intelligence** | AI brings expertise; it knows the domain from data across many customers | Benchmarks, recommendations and decisions grounded in proprietary vertical data; answers experts trust | Claims (with customers) of models trained or tuned on proprietary domain data, cross-customer benchmarks, expert-grade answers, regulatory-grade accuracy |
| 6 | **System of Understanding** | The product understands how the work and the domain behave, anticipates and acts | It predicts, plans and runs the domain's work; customers can't imagine operating without it | Rare. The product is the place decisions are made, not just recorded; evidence of anticipating events and acting across the whole workflow |

The curve bends between levels 4 and 5: software stops recording the work and starts doing the work above it. Proprietary data starts building the next moat at level 3.

## Rules for placing a feature

- **Shipped beats announced.** Beta and waitlists count as half a level at most; announcements and roadmaps don't count. Say which.
- **Used beats shipped.** A customer quote, case study or metric is the strongest evidence.
- **Sourced or not placed.** Every placement cites a page. No source: write "not placed" and why.
- **Confidence**: high (shipped, documented, customer evidence), medium (shipped, documented), low (beta, marketing claims only).
- **Don't flatter the team's own product.** Apply the same rules to it.

## Illustrative examples (for the talk; check before quoting)

| Level | Examples |
|---|---|
| 1 System of Record | Epic patient records · Procore project records |
| 2 AI Assisted | Gmail Smart Compose · Clio Duo summarising a legal matter |
| 3 AI Directed | Microsoft 365 Copilot drafting from your files · Shopify Sidekick doing a merchant's task on request |
| 4 AI Delegated | Intercom Fin resolving support conversations · Abridge drafting the clinical note a doctor signs · EvenUp drafting demand letters |
| 5 AI Domain Intelligence | Harvey for law firms · OpenEvidence for clinicians |
| 6 System of Understanding | Few yet. Closest today: platforms like Tempus in oncology that combine years of domain data with recommendations |
